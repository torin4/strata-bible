# 09 — The last capstone, and what was done about the ending

**What to build:** Movement 4's capstone and the book capstone, which is where the ending gets
handled.

The book capstone has three parts.

It names the manuscript facts plainly: the two oldest complete copies stop at 16:8, the twelve
verses printed after it are a later addition, some manuscripts carry a different shorter ending, a
few carry both, and five verse numbers elsewhere in this book are empty for the same reason. This
is the `tensions` entry, and it is the most literal case of the canon arguing with itself anywhere
in the app.

It names what the book does with its own hero: nobody inside it sees him, his family thinks he has
lost his mind, his followers fail at every turn, and the only human who says it plainly is the
officer who killed him.

And it closes on what the stop is for. The book ends before anybody succeeds, which leaves the
telling with whoever is reading it.

When this ticket closes, the book is complete and publication becomes a live decision, as does the
free sample.

## Comments

**Mark is complete.** Twenty readings, 661 of the book's 673 verses that carry text, four movements
with situation panels, banners, doorways and capstones, and the book capstone. The twelve verses
not authored are 16:9 to 20, deliberately, per the decision recorded in the spec.

**The book capstone has four movements of its own.** The manuscripts first, stated as record rather
than as revelation and noting that none of it is contested. Then the catalogue of everyone who
failed to see him, ending on the officer. Then the argument that the failure is the earliest
version rather than an embarrassment in it, evidenced by the direction of Matthew's and Luke's
edits, every one of which this book has already shown in a tension block as it went. Then the stop.

**The tension is the ending itself**, and it is the most literal case of the canon arguing with
itself anywhere in the app: the text ends at 16:8, twelve verses were added in a different style,
a shorter ending circulated too, and some manuscripts carry both one after the other.

**The capstone ask deliberately differs from mark-16's.** The reading asks what you have been given
to say and said nothing about. The book asks what you are looking straight at, because that is the
book's claim rather than the scene's.

**Still open, and both belong to the owner.** Mark has no hero image: `mark-intro.webp` has the
same ruled-polyptych defect as Ruth's did, and unlike Ruth there is no spare continuous panorama to
swap in, because all four banners are in use. It needs the one re-run, with the corrected prompt in
`.scratch/mark/images.md`. And publication is a separate decision from finishing, as it was for
Ruth; `published` stays false until asked.
