# 02 — Author Mark 1:1–20, the beginning

**What to build:** The first sitting of the New Testament in this app, and the reading everything
after it is written against.

Three scenes. The beginning (1:1–8), the tearing (1:9–13), and follow me (1:14–20).

The book opens with no birth, no genealogy and no childhood, and its first sentence is a
provocation rather than a devotional opening: gospel was the word for an imperial announcement,
and son of God was a title Augustus used. That is the load-bearing misreading and it belongs on
scene one, which has the scripture to carry it.

Scene two holds the tension. Jesus is baptised by John in a baptism the text has just called one
of repentance for the forgiveness of sins, and the three gospels written after this one are all
visibly uncomfortable with the sentence. Matthew has John protest, Luke reports it without putting
John in the scene, and the fourth gospel drops the baptism entirely. The same scene carries Mark's
verb for the heavens, which is the one for tearing, and which he uses again at the temple curtain
in chapter 15. It also gives the wilderness as Mark gives it: two verses, no dialogue, no three
temptations, and wild animals nobody else mentions.

Scene three carries the turn. Zebedee has hired men, so what the fishermen walk away from is a
working business, not destitution.

This is the voice gate for the New Testament. Stop here for review.

## Comments

**The misreading is the imperial reading of the first sentence**, and it goes on scene one because
that scene has 158 words of scripture to carry it. Gospel and son of God were both Roman political
vocabulary before they were religious vocabulary, and a first sentence that borrows both and
attaches them to a labourer the state will execute is a provocation, not an invocation.

**The tension is the baptism.** Mark says plainly that he was baptised by John, four verses after
saying that John's baptism was for the forgiveness of sins, and the three gospels written later
are all visibly careful with that sentence. Setting the four side by side is the cheapest possible
demonstration of what this app is for, and it needs no argument from us: the four texts do it.

**The wilderness is given as Mark gives it.** Two verses, no dialogue, no three temptations, and
wild animals that appear in no other account. Most readers will find their memory of the scene is
not in the earliest gospel, which is the point.

**Density ran hot and was trimmed once.** Scene two came in at 2.1x, with the tearing said twice,
once in the ground note and again at the end of the meaning. Cutting the second telling took it to
1.7x. The reading sits at 1.7x, 1.7x, 1.6x overall, which is above the book norm of about 1.5x and
is justified here: the imperial context, the composite quotation and the manuscript situation all
have to be established once, in the first reading of a new testament, and they do not need saying
again in chapter two.

**Turn is `names`, on the closing scene**, per the house rule. It does not preach the summons. It
names doing the arithmetic afterward rather than at the time.
