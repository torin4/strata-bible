# 08 — Movement 4, handed over

**What to build:** The passion, and the stop.

Movement 4 (`handed-over`, chapters 14–16), four readings, plus movement 3's capstone and doorway.

`mark-14a` is the woman with the jar, the supper, Gethsemane and the arrest. Mark's Gethsemane is
the least composed portrait of him in the four gospels, and the reading leaves it that way.

`mark-14b` is the trial and Peter in the courtyard, which is another sandwich: Mark cuts between
the man being condemned for saying who he is and the man outside swearing he does not know him.

`mark-15` is the sentence, the crucifixion, the cry from the cross and the centurion. The cry is
the hardest line in the book and it is not repaired here with a line from another gospel. The
centurion's sentence is the book's own answer to the question it has been asking since chapter one,
and Mark chose to put it in the mouth of the man who ran the execution.

`mark-16` is 16:1–8 and stops there. The women say nothing to anyone, because they were afraid, and
that is the last sentence of the book.
