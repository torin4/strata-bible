# 08 — The epilogue, the last capstones, and the book's own ending

**What to build:** `ecc-12`, movement 3's capstone, and the book capstone.

`ecc-12` is 12:9–14, and it is a different voice. It speaks of the Teacher in the third person,
praises his work from outside it, warns that of making many books there is no end, and closes on
fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man. After twelve chapters of
vapour that lands like a door closing. Whether it is a later hand making a dangerous book safe
enough to keep, or the author signing his own work, is genuinely argued, and the reading says so
rather than choosing.

The book capstone carries the argument. That this book is in the canon at all is the most
interesting fact about it: a text that says the dead know nothing, that a man has no advantage over
a beast, and that the righteous perish while the wicked prosper, was kept, copied and read. Not
because it was tamed, though the epilogue tried, but because somebody decided the collection needed
a voice that would say this. The tension is the epilogue against the book.

And it closes on the sentence the book actually recommends, which is not despair: eat your bread,
drink your wine, do your work, and love somebody, because the day is short and you do not get to
know how it turns out.

When this ticket closes the book is complete and publication becomes a live decision.
