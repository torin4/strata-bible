# 08 — The epilogue, the last capstones, and the book's own ending

**What to build:** `ecc-12`, movement 3's capstone, and the book capstone.

`ecc-12` is 12:9–14, and it is a different voice. It speaks of the Teacher in the third person,
praises his work from outside it, warns that of making many books there is no end, and closes on
fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man. After twelve chapters of
vapour that lands like a door closing. Whether it is a later hand making a dangerous book safe
enough to keep, or the author signing his own work, is genuinely argued, and the reading says so
rather than choosing.

The book capstone carries the argument. That this book is in the canon at all is the most
interesting fact about it: a text that says the dead know nothing, that a man has no advantage over
a beast, and that the righteous perish while the wicked prosper, was kept, copied and read. Not
because it was tamed, though the epilogue tried, but because somebody decided the collection needed
a voice that would say this. The tension is the epilogue against the book.

And it closes on the sentence the book actually recommends, which is not despair: eat your bread,
drink your wine, do your work, and love somebody, because the day is short and you do not get to
know how it turns out.

When this ticket closes the book is complete and publication becomes a live decision.

## Comments

**The epilogue is given both readings and no verdict.** A later editor framing twelve dangerous
chapters with a summary orthodox enough to keep, or the author signing his own work with a judgment
clause consistent with the one he attached to the young man two chapters earlier. What the reading
does settle is the effect: after twelve chapters of vapour it lands like a door closing on a room
somebody had just opened a window in.

**The epilogue-against-the-book tension moved to the book capstone**, where the ticket said it
belonged. It had been duplicated in `ecc-12`, which pushed that six-verse reading to 2.5x on density.
Removing the duplicate fixed both problems at once, which is usually the sign that the density gate
is measuring something real rather than just counting.

**The book capstone argues from the book's presence in the canon**, per the ticket. That a text
saying these things was kept, copied, argued over and read aloud is the most interesting fact about
it, and it was not kept by being tamed: the epilogue is six verses and the twelve chapters in front
of it are not adjusted to fit.

**It closes on the permission, not the bleakness.** Six times, always immediately after the hardest
sentence in the paragraph. Offered as the portion, which is this book's own word for what a person
actually gets.

**One structural note for the record.** 210 of 222 verses are authored. The twelve absent are inside
the two saying-clusters, which are selections by design, and the expansion fill does not complete
list-form passages, so those chapters cannot be revealed in full the way a prose chapter can. That
is a known property of the kind rather than a defect here, and it is the same for the Leviticus
fixture.
