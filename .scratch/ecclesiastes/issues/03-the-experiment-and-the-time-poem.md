# 03 — Author Ecclesiastes 2 to 4, and close the first movement

**What to build:** Three sittings and the movement 1 capstone.

`ecc-2` is the experiment: he tries pleasure, building, wealth and wisdom deliberately and reports
the result. It ends on the book's first statement of its own answer, that there is nothing better
than to eat and drink and find enjoyment in one's work, which arrives long before most readers
expect it and is easy to miss.

`ecc-3` carries the time poem, 3:1–8, as a `poem` in `poetry` form. This is the most quoted passage
in the book and the one most worn smooth, so the apparatus has to earn its place: what the poem is
doing is not consoling, it is listing, and half the pairs are things nobody wants. The chapter then
puts eternity in the human heart and says a man has no advantage over a beast, and that carries the
movement's hardest tension.

`ecc-4` is oppression, rivalry and the cord of three strands, and it is where the book's cold eye
lands on other people rather than on the self.

The capstone closes on what the movement establishes and does not pre-empt the answer that comes
in movement 3.
