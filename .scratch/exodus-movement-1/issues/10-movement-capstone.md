# 10 — Movement capstone

**What to build:** The look-back that closes the movement. A reader who finishes the Song reaches
it, and it carries the meaning of the whole stretch, which no single reading holds on its own.

Its work is to return to the cost. The movement has been a deliverance, and it has been paid for
by dead children and a drowned army, and the reader has been asked to sing about it. The capstone
holds those together and declines to resolve them. It carries the internal tensions that only
surface at the level of the whole, which is what the capstone's tension field exists for.

It closes the movement and nothing more. There is no doorway, because the next movement does not
exist yet, and the content validator fails a doorway pointing at a movement that is not there.
The doorway arrives with the movement it points to.

It must not read as a book-level wrap-up. Exodus is one movement of four so far, and the reader
should be left at the far shore of the sea with the road to the mountain still ahead, not handed
a conclusion the book has not reached.

When this ticket is done, movement 1 is complete: eleven readings, seven sittings, a situation
panel, a composition panel, and a capstone, all unpublished and all green.

## Comments

**Form matched to the existing capstones**: three paragraphs, one tension, no ask, three sources.
Genesis's movement capstones run 200 to 257 words; this one came in at 294 and was trimmed to
267. Nothing gated that, since the density check only measures passages, so it was held by hand
the way the ticket-06 lesson says to.

**The synthesis is the shape of the whole stretch, not a recap.** God absent for four chapters,
then a reluctant man, then an attempt that makes things worse and a people turning on their
rescuer. Nothing in this movement happens quickly and the book appears to want it that way.

**The cost is returned to and left standing.** Egypt loses its firstborn, mirroring the boys in
the Nile at the start, and naming that logic is not the same as being at peace with it. The
capstone says outright that the movement gives Israel a song and gives Egypt no funeral, and
that the asymmetry is in the text rather than smuggled in by a reader.

**The whole-level tension is the one no single reading could carry**: the exodus as God taking
the side of the enslaved, against the same canon refusing to make that side permanent or
exclusive, with Amos 9:7 putting the Philistines and the Arameans in the same sentence. The
partial answer offered is what Israel did with the memory, that the law keeps turning it into an
obligation toward the foreigner rather than a privilege. Offered, not used to close the question.

**It does not read as an ending.** It closes on being out but not home, the mountain still ahead,
and thirst waiting three days past the singing. Exodus carries no book-level capstone and will
not until all forty chapters are authored, which is asserted in the tests so a later movement
cannot quietly acquire one.

**Movement 1 is complete**: eleven readings, eight sittings, three grounded, a situation panel and
a capstone. Only the book composition panel, ticket 05, is outstanding.
