# 08 — Author `ex-7` through `ex-12`

**What to build:** The hardest content in the movement. The plague cycle as one reading, and
Passover as the next.

The plague reading is where the movement's moral problem is concentrated rather than distributed.
God hardens the heart of the man who might otherwise have relented, and then every firstborn in
Egypt dies. An app that promises to name where Scripture argues with itself and to refuse tidy
morals either handles this honestly here or forfeits the promise for the whole book.

Three specific obligations:

The reading carries the movement's load-bearing misreading, on the hardening. Name the one that
does the most damage and state plainly why it fails, without burying the reader in caveats.

It carries a tension that sets the claim this passage makes, that God's justice frees the
enslaved, against another voice inside the canon that pushes back: Ezekiel, where a child does not
bear the father's guilt, and Amos, where the exodus turns out not to be Israel's private
possession at all. This is the field that exists so Scripture is not flattened into one voice.
Use it properly.

Both the plague reading and Passover make a demand on the reader rather than naming something
already true of them. The plague reading because a hardened heart is a description of how refusal
calcifies rather than a divine puppet show. Passover because it is a meal that remembers a cost,
and remembering the cost is the demand.

Do not resolve the problem in these readings. The movement's capstone returns to it and also
declines to resolve it. That is deliberate.
