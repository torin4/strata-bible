# 08 — Author `ex-7` through `ex-12`

**What to build:** The hardest content in the movement. The plague cycle as one reading, and
Passover as the next.

The plague reading is where the movement's moral problem is concentrated rather than distributed.
God hardens the heart of the man who might otherwise have relented, and then every firstborn in
Egypt dies. An app that promises to name where Scripture argues with itself and to refuse tidy
morals either handles this honestly here or forfeits the promise for the whole book.

Three specific obligations:

The reading carries the movement's load-bearing misreading, on the hardening. Name the one that
does the most damage and state plainly why it fails, without burying the reader in caveats.

It carries a tension that sets the claim this passage makes, that God's justice frees the
enslaved, against another voice inside the canon that pushes back: Ezekiel, where a child does not
bear the father's guilt, and Amos, where the exodus turns out not to be Israel's private
possession at all. This is the field that exists so Scripture is not flattened into one voice.
Use it properly.

Both the plague reading and Passover make a demand on the reader rather than naming something
already true of them. The plague reading because a hardened heart is a description of how refusal
calcifies rather than a divine puppet show. Passover because it is a meal that remembers a cost,
and remembering the cost is the demand.

Do not resolve the problem in these readings. The movement's capstone returns to it and also
declines to resolve it. That is deliberate.

## Comments

**The escalation is built into the scene titles and the argument, not just the sequence.** The
river (the first sign is an argument about who the Nile belongs to, and the magicians match it).
Frogs and gnats (Egypt's experts can add to a problem, never remove it, and they break first).
Hail (the first confession, worth nothing, reversed the moment the sky clears). Locusts and
darkness (the hardening stated outright, the officials broken, the sun god's country blind).
The last word (the announcement, and the silence after it).

**The misreading sits on scene four**, where the text itself says I have hardened his heart. The
argument is the verb order: through the early rounds Pharaoh hardens his own heart, and only
later is God named as doing it. Not a man denied the chance to repent, but a man who took the
chance nine times and used it to say no. This is also where `ex-1`'s claim, that the book is
about a system rather than a villain, pays off: the hardening is a description of what repeated
refusal does to a person, which is why the turn can make a demand rather than describe a tyrant.

**The tension sits on scene five**, where the firstborn are announced, because that is where the
cost lands. Claim: God's justice frees the enslaved and the cost falls on the nation that
enslaved them. Counter: Ezekiel 18:20 refuses to let a son bear his father's guilt, and Amos 9:7
refuses to let Israel own the exodus at all. Left unresolved, for ticket 10.

**Both turns demand.** With `ex-1`'s two, that makes four `claims` turns in the movement out of
thirteen. The other nine name. The register holds.

**Density.** Every new passage came in between 0.57x and 1.76x. One scene needed a four-word trim
to clear 1.8x, which the gate caught rather than a reviewer.

**Structural edits were scoped to the reading this time**, after the ex-1 splice mistake in
ticket 07. `ex-12` split into two scenes cleanly; all 847 verses still verify and the reveal
