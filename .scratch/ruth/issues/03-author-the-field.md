# 03 — Author Ruth 2, the field

**What to build:** The second sitting, and the first chapter of the rebuilding.

Ruth 2: a widow with no land asks to go and pick up what the harvesters drop, and happens onto
the field of a man from her dead husband's clan. The ground note carries the gleaning law from
Leviticus 19 and Deuteronomy 24, which is welfare written as a restriction on the landowner
rather than as charity: he is forbidden to harvest his own corners and forbidden to go back for
what he missed.

The scene to resist sentimentalising is the meeting. Boaz is generous and he is also a man of
standing talking to a foreign woman who has no protection, and he tells his men not to touch her,
which tells you what she was walking into. The reading should let both be true.

The chapter's own hinge is Naomi hearing whose field it was, and using the word `kinsman-redeemer`
for the first time. That is the plot arriving. It should not be smoothed into coincidence or into
providence: the narrator says only that she happened to come to that part of the field.

## Comments

**The misreading is on Boaz.** Read as warmth alone, chapter 2 becomes a nice story about a kind
man, and what Ruth is actually doing disappears: working in public, in front of men, all day, to be
noticed enough to be allowed to keep working. He is generous and everything he grants is a favour
he could withdraw. Both are true and the reading holds both.

**The wing and the corner.** Boaz blesses her under the wings of the God of Israel in 2:12, and the
Hebrew word for wing is the same word used for the corner of a garment, which is what Ruth asks him
to spread over her in 3:9. The ground note plants it here and the meaning in chapter 3 collects it.
That is the book's own construction, not ours.
