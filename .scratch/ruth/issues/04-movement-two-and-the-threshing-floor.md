# 04 — Movement 2 declared, and the threshing floor

**What to build:** The book's hinge, and the movement that holds it.

Movement 2 (`the-redeemer`, Ruth 3–4) is declared with its situation panel, which is where the
legal machinery gets explained: the go'el or kinsman-redeemer in Leviticus 25, the levirate
obligation in Deuteronomy 25, and the fact that what happens in this book is not exactly either
one. The book appears to be constructing its own mechanism, and saying so is more honest than
tidying it into a statute that fits.

Ruth 3 is the chapter most often sanitised. Naomi sends her daughter-in-law, washed and perfumed,
to lie down at night beside a man who has been drinking, and tells her he will say what to do. The
Hebrew is deliberately ambiguous in ways translations settle. The reading names the ambiguity
rather than resolving it in either direction, and names what Ruth is risking, since a foreign
widow caught on a threshing floor has no recourse at all.

Movement 1's doorway is added here, because a doorway cannot exist until the movement it points
at does.
