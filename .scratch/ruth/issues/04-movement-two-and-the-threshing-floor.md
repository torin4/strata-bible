# 04 — Movement 2 declared, and the threshing floor

**What to build:** The book's hinge, and the movement that holds it.

Movement 2 (`the-redeemer`, Ruth 3–4) is declared with its situation panel, which is where the
legal machinery gets explained: the go'el or kinsman-redeemer in Leviticus 25, the levirate
obligation in Deuteronomy 25, and the fact that what happens in this book is not exactly either
one. The book appears to be constructing its own mechanism, and saying so is more honest than
tidying it into a statute that fits.

Ruth 3 is the chapter most often sanitised. Naomi sends her daughter-in-law, washed and perfumed,
to lie down at night beside a man who has been drinking, and tells her he will say what to do. The
Hebrew is deliberately ambiguous in ways translations settle. The reading names the ambiguity
rather than resolving it in either direction, and names what Ruth is risking, since a foreign
widow caught on a threshing floor has no recourse at all.

Movement 1's doorway is added here, because a doorway cannot exist until the movement it points
at does.

## Comments

**The ambiguity is named and not settled.** The word for feet is used elsewhere as a euphemism, the
verb for uncovering carries the same freight, and lie down is the ordinary word for both sleeping
and sex. Translations pick a side. The narrator did not, and the misreading block says so plainly:
the suggestion is in the Hebrew, not in the reader. What the book is unambiguous about is the risk
she is running and that what she asked for was not a night.

**The situation panel says the law does not quite fit.** Redemption in Leviticus 25 covers land and
relatives sold into debt. The levirate in Deuteronomy 25 covers brothers. Boaz is neither, and what
happens at the gate combines the two in a case no statute covers. Whether the book is describing a
custom otherwise unrecorded or constructing what it thinks the law should have said is genuinely
argued, and saying so beats tidying it.

**The closing scene is where the book answers chapter 1.** Boaz sends her home with six measures of
barley and one sentence: do not go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed. Empty is Naomi's word
from the street. He heard what she called herself and answered it with about thirty pounds of grain
and no comment. That is the turn.
