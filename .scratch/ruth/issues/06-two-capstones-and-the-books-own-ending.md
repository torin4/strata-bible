# 06 — Two capstones, and the book's own ending

**What to build:** The look-backs. Two movement capstones and the book capstone, which is where
Ruth's argument actually lives.

Movement 1's capstone closes on emptiness and the first grain, and declines to promise what
chapters 3 and 4 will do. Movement 2's capstone closes on the claim being pressed and settled.

The book capstone carries the thesis, and it has three parts. Almost nothing supernatural happens
in this book: God acts once, in a single clause about conception, and the entire rescue is
otherwise carried out by people deciding to be decent on purpose. The book never restores what it
took. And it ends by making a Moabite woman David's great-grandmother, which is the canon arguing
with Deuteronomy 23 and with Ezra and Nehemiah in the form of a story, and it carries the
`tensions` entry that says so.

When this ticket closes, the book is complete and publication becomes a live decision.
