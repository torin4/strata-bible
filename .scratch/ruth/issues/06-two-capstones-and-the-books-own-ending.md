# 06 — Two capstones, and the book's own ending

**What to build:** The look-backs. Two movement capstones and the book capstone, which is where
Ruth's argument actually lives.

Movement 1's capstone closes on emptiness and the first grain, and declines to promise what
chapters 3 and 4 will do. Movement 2's capstone closes on the claim being pressed and settled.

The book capstone carries the thesis, and it has three parts. Almost nothing supernatural happens
in this book: God acts once, in a single clause about conception, and the entire rescue is
otherwise carried out by people deciding to be decent on purpose. The book never restores what it
took. And it ends by making a Moabite woman David's great-grandmother, which is the canon arguing
with Deuteronomy 23 and with Ezra and Nehemiah in the form of a story, and it carries the
`tensions` entry that says so.

When this ticket closes, the book is complete and publication becomes a live decision.

## Comments

**The book capstone has the three parts the ticket asked for**, in the order that makes the
argument land. How little God does: two acts in 85 verses, one of them heard about second-hand in a
foreign country, and a key word that appears three times and always means somebody doing more than
they owed. What is not restored: Naomi never takes back what she said in the street, and the book
never stages the moment where she sees it differently. And the last line, where a Moabite is named
as David's great-grandmother without comment, which is the tension and the argument at once.

**Movement 1's capstone deliberately promises nothing.** Two chapters in, the arithmetic has barely
moved, and the honest look-back is that nothing was restored and two people did more than they had
to. Pre-empting the second half would have cost the book its shape, which is the same note the
Joseph capstone in Genesis had to be corrected for.

**One deviation from the spec, recorded.** The spec said the book capstone should carry the
Moabite tension. It does, and `ruth-1` carries it too, on the vow. That is deliberate rather than
duplicated: the reader meets the law when Ruth declares herself into Israel, and meets the
consequence when the genealogy names her. The same tension at both ends is the book's own frame.

**Ruth is complete: 85 of 85 verses authored, four sittings, two movements, every gate green.**
Publication is now a live decision and is not taken here. `published` stays false and `COMING_SOON`
stays empty until the owner says otherwise.
