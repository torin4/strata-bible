# 02 — Author Ruth 1, the road

**What to build:** The first sitting of the book, and the reading everything after it is written
against.

Ruth 1 in three scenes: the emptying (1:1–7), go back (1:8–18), and the vow and the name
(1:19–22). A famine takes a family out of Bethlehem, which means house of bread, into Moab, which
is the wrong country. Inside five verses the husband and both sons are dead. The middle scene is
Naomi on the road trying to send her daughters-in-law home, one of whom goes and one of whom will
not. The last scene is the arrival, and a woman refusing her own name in front of the whole town.

Two things this reading has to hold, and they are the reason this book is being written before
the gospel.

The deaths are not a punishment. A long tradition reads the move to Moab as the sin the deaths
repay. The narrator says only that there was a famine and a man went.

Naomi's bitterness is not a failure of faith awaiting correction. She says the hand of the LORD
has gone out against her and asks to be called Mara, and nobody in the book contradicts her, then
or ever. That is the load-bearing misreading of the reading, and it belongs on the closing scene.

The middle scene carries the tension: Ruth declares herself into Israel, and Deuteronomy 23:3
bars Moabites from the assembly to the tenth generation.

One turn, on the closing scene, mode `names`. The reading closes with a `prayer` in `meditate`
mode rather than only an ask, because a chapter that ends on "I went away full, but the LORD has
brought me back empty" should leave a reader with something to carry.

This is the voice gate. Stop here for review before Ruth 2 is written.

## Comments

**The misreading moved from the closing scene to the middle one.** As specced it sat on
1:19–22 with the meaning, the turn, the ask and the prayer, and the density gate failed the
passage at 1.9x: 208 words of apparatus on 108 words of scripture. Moving it is not a
concession to the gate. Naomi says the hand of the LORD has gone out against her at 1:13, which
is where the misreading is answering, and the closing scene now runs at 1.6x and lands quiet:
scripture, a short meaning, the turn, and something to carry. The chapter should not end with the
reader being argued with.

**What the reading refuses to do.** It does not read the deaths in Moab as punishment, and it says
so. It does not treat Naomi's bitterness as a low point awaiting correction, and it names the fact
that nobody in the book, including God, ever contradicts her. Both of those are the reason this
book was written before the gospel.

**The turn.** One, on the closing scene, mode `names`, per the house rule Exodus established. It
does not tell the reader anything about grief. It names being asked how you are in the street.

**First use of `prayer` outside the Song of the Sea.** Mode `meditate`. A chapter that ends on
"I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty" should leave a reader with something to
carry rather than only a question to answer.

**The tension is on the vow**, where Ruth declares herself into Israel, set against Deuteronomy
23:3 and the campaign in Ezra and Nehemiah, with Ruth 4:17 named as the book's own answer. That is
the thread the book capstone picks up in ticket 06.
