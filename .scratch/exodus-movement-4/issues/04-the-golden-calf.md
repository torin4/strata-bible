# 04 — The golden calf

**What to build:** Exodus 32, the movement's hardest chapter, told without protection for anyone
in it.

Moses is up the mountain too long, the people ask Aaron for gods to go before them, and Aaron
makes one out of their jewellery and builds an altar in front of it. His account of it afterward,
that he threw the gold in the fire and out came this calf, is one of the great evasions in
scripture and should be allowed to be funny before it is anything else. This is the same Aaron
whose priestly clothing was specified in the previous reading, and the reading should not quietly
protect him.

Then Moses arguing God out of destroying the people, which is worth showing as an argument rather
than a formality: he appeals to God's reputation among the Egyptians and to promises made to dead
men, and the text says God relented.

Then the tablets broken at the foot of the mountain, and then the part most retellings omit. Moses
calls for whoever is on the LORD's side, the Levites come to him, and he sends them through the
camp with swords, and about three thousand people die, and the chapter treats it as faithfulness
that earns them priestly standing.

That passage is harder than the servant laws in the covenant movement, for a specific reason: the
mode for law that does not bind the reader is not available to it. This is not legislation that
can be marked as expired. It is a narrative the book approves of. It stays in the reading, it is
named as difficult, it is not justified, and it is not resolved here. Both capstones will have to
live with it rather than dispose of it.
