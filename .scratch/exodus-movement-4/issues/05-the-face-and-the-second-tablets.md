# 05 — The face, and the second tablets

**What to build:** Two readings, Exodus 33 and Exodus 34, which belong together.

Chapter 33 is the most intimate passage in the book. God says he will send an angel ahead of them
but will not go up among them himself, and Moses refuses the offer: if your presence does not go
with us, do not send us up from here. Then he asks for more than he has been given. Show me your
glory. The answer is the one the whole Bible keeps circling. You cannot see my face, for no one
may see me and live. But there is a cleft in a rock, and a hand over it while the glory passes,
and afterward, my back. The book's closest approach to God is a description of what was seen going
away.

Chapter 34 is the second set of tablets, cut by Moses this time rather than given, and the formula
that follows is the sentence the rest of the Bible quotes about God more than any other:
compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion and faithfulness,
forgiving wickedness, and by no means leaving the guilty unpunished, visiting the iniquity of the
fathers on their children to the third and fourth generation.

Both halves of that are in one breath and the reading has to hold them there. The collision is
addressed in the meaning layer of chapter 34, and the tension that names it is deliberately NOT
placed here. The counter-voice it needs has already been used twice in this book, and a third use
at passage level would read as a tic. It belongs on the book capstone, where a book-length
argument about whether guilt travels can actually be held.
