# 03 — The priest, and the craftsman

**What to build:** Exodus 28 to 31, selected: who wears what, and who makes it.

The priest's clothing is specified in more detail than most of the furniture, and the detail that
matters is what he carries. The names of the twelve tribes are cut into stones on his shoulders
and worn over his heart, so that he goes into the presence carrying the people on his body. That
is the reading's centre.

Then Bezalel. God names a craftsman and says he has filled him with the Spirit of God, with skill
and ability and knowledge in all kinds of crafts. It is the first time in the Bible anyone is said
to be filled with God's Spirit, and it is not a prophet or a priest or a king. It is a man who
works metal, stone and wood. A reading that has just spent chapters on specifications is the right
place to notice that the book thinks making things is a spiritual office.

The stretch closes with the sabbath restated, which is worth keeping: even the building of the
sanctuary stops on the seventh day.

One passage per chapter, since the reading spans several.

## Comments

**Four scenes, one per chapter.** The vestments, the ordination, the census tax, and Bezalel.

**The centre is what the priest carries.** Twelve tribe names cut into stones, six and six on his
shoulders and twelve over his heart, worn whenever he goes in. The job is not to represent God to
the people. It is to carry the people, by name, on his body, into the presence, and to bear the
guilt of whatever they get wrong in the offering.

**The census tax turned out to be the surprise of the reading.** Half a shekel from every man, and
the rich may not give more nor the poor less, in a code that elsewhere scales offerings to what a
person can afford. The point is not affordability but equivalence: the payment stands for a life,
and no life is worth more of it than another.

**Bezalel gets the turn.** The first person in the Bible said to be filled with the Spirit of God
is not a prophet, priest or king but a man who works gold, stone and wood, and what he is filled
with is skill. Four chapters of specification end by calling craftsmanship a gift of God's own
Spirit, and then by restating the sabbath over the one project that could plausibly claim exemption.
