# 07 — Two capstones, and the last doorway

**What to build:** The end of the movement, the end of the book, and the last link in the chain.

Three things land together because none of them is complete without the others.

The movement capstone holds Exodus 25 to 40: a tent, a failure, and a rebuilding. Its work is to
say what the tabernacle is for, that a God who rescued a people arranged to travel with them, and
to carry forward what chapter 32 did without disposing of it.

The book capstone looks over all forty chapters. From a policy of drowning boys in a river to a
tent so full of glory that the man who built it cannot go inside. It is not a summary of movement
4 and must not read as one. The whole argument of the book is available to it: that a rescue is
not the same as an arrival, that the people leave Egypt in the first movement and spend the other
three learning what having been rescued obliges them to.

It also carries the tension deferred from chapter 34, deliberately held back for this. The
character formula holds compassion and generational punishment in one sentence, and the canon
argues with the second half of it in more than one place. That is a book-length argument about
whether guilt travels, and this look-back is the first thing large enough to hold it.

Movement 3 gains its doorway, pointing at movement 4. Movement 4 gains none and never will: it is
the last movement of the book.

Verified behaviour worth relying on: the reader gives the book capstone its own screen one step
past the last scene, while the movement capstone renders inline on that scene. Both appear on the
final reading without competing. That is what was built when the Joseph capstone was found to be
pre-empting the Genesis closing note, and it means no sequencing work is needed here.

When this ticket is done, Exodus is a complete book of forty chapters, still unpublished. The
publishing decision is recorded in the spec and is not taken here.
