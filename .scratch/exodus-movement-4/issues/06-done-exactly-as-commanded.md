# 06 — Done exactly as commanded

**What to build:** One reading over Exodus 35 to 40 whose subject is the repetition itself.

These six chapters restate the seven that preceded the golden calf, almost word for word, with the
verbs changed from you are to make into he made. Same dimensions, same materials, same order.
There is no way to author them at full apparatus without making a reader meet the same
specifications twice, and no way to drop them without losing the end of the book, because the last
five verses of Exodus are in here.

So the reading is about the repetition. It sets a small number of paired verses against their
originals, the instruction beside its execution, so the claim is shown rather than asserted. Then
it makes the argument: the calf falls between the two blocks. The covenant was broken in the gap,
and the construction narrative is the record that what was broken got rebuilt exactly as
specified, down to the loops on the curtains. A book that wanted to be brief could have written
"and they did everything as commanded" in a single verse. It spent six chapters instead, and it
spent them immediately after the failure.

The other readings of the repetition are worth naming rather than suppressing: that it is scribal
duplication, or a liturgical recitation meant to be heard rather than read. The reading should say
that the "it is the point" reading is the only one that explains why the duplicate block sits
where it sits.

It closes on the last five verses. The cloud covers the tent, the glory fills it so completely
that Moses cannot go in, and the book ends with the cloud lifting or settling to tell a people
whether to travel or stay. That is the final image of Exodus and it should be given room.

## Comments

**Six passages, one per chapter, deliberately uneven.** Chapters 35, 36 and 40 carry the weight;
37, 38 and 39 are kept sparse because they are the "here it is again" chapters and the plainness is
the argument. Chapter 37 has a ground note and nothing else, which is the only passage in the book
authored that way on purpose.

**The pairing is shown, not asserted.** Chapter 37's ground note sets 25:10 against 37:1 in the
text: they are to construct an ark of acacia wood, two and a half cubits long, becomes Bezalel went
on to construct the ark of acacia wood, two and a half cubits long. Same dimensions, same
materials, same order, one word changed.

**The argument lands on chapter 39.** The refrain, just as the LORD had commanded Moses, is a
verdict rather than filler, and where the book puts it is the whole case: the calf falls between
the plans and the building, so this is the record that what was broken got rebuilt exactly to
specification. The alternatives are named rather than suppressed, scribal duplication and
liturgical recitation, with the observation that neither explains why the duplicate block sits
precisely on the far side of the failure.

**Two things in these chapters are new rather than repeated, and both got kept.** The people
bringing so much that they have to be restrained, which is the only place in the Bible where
generosity has to be stopped. And the bronze basin made from the mirrors of the women who served at
the entrance, which the instructions never specified. The inventory that follows counts everything
in talents and shekels; that verse counts something the inventory cannot.

**The ending is given room.** The cloud settles, the glory fills the tent, and the man who built it
cannot go in. Not punished, simply shut out because the place is too full. Then the book stops
mid-journey on an instruction about watching a cloud to know whether to travel today.
