# 06 — Done exactly as commanded

**What to build:** One reading over Exodus 35 to 40 whose subject is the repetition itself.

These six chapters restate the seven that preceded the golden calf, almost word for word, with the
verbs changed from you are to make into he made. Same dimensions, same materials, same order.
There is no way to author them at full apparatus without making a reader meet the same
specifications twice, and no way to drop them without losing the end of the book, because the last
five verses of Exodus are in here.

So the reading is about the repetition. It sets a small number of paired verses against their
originals, the instruction beside its execution, so the claim is shown rather than asserted. Then
it makes the argument: the calf falls between the two blocks. The covenant was broken in the gap,
and the construction narrative is the record that what was broken got rebuilt exactly as
specified, down to the loops on the curtains. A book that wanted to be brief could have written
"and they did everything as commanded" in a single verse. It spent six chapters instead, and it
spent them immediately after the failure.

The other readings of the repetition are worth naming rather than suppressing: that it is scribal
duplication, or a liturgical recitation meant to be heard rather than read. The reading should say
that the "it is the point" reading is the only one that explains why the duplicate block sits
where it sits.

It closes on the last five verses. The cloud covers the tent, the glory fills it so completely
that Moses cannot go in, and the book ends with the cloud lifting or settling to tell a people
whether to travel or stay. That is the final image of Exodus and it should be given room.
